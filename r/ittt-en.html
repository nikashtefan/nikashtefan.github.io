Where AI could take manual work off

idea 1

A quote draft from the client's request

  1. A request comes in by form, email or Telegram: model or specs, quantity, budget
  2. AI matches it to your catalog and supplier price lists, picks 2–3 options and adds price with 20% VAT, warranty and lead time
  3. The manager gets a ready quote draft in your template
  4. The quote goes out in minutes, the manager only checks and sends
Takes off: picking models by hand and copying prices and lead times into the quote
idea 2

Big multi-item requests sorted line by line

  1. The client sends a list, like 25 PCs, 50 monitors and peripherals, in Excel, PDF or a message
  2. AI reads every line, finds matching models and marks what is in stock and what needs ordering from Russia, China or the UAE
  3. The manager gets one table with options and lead times for each line
  4. A large request gets an answer the same day without hours of sorting
Takes off: going through long spec lists item by item
idea 3

Reminders for regular clients

  1. Order history of regular clients, for example MFPs and the toner they use
  2. AI looks at when consumables or a refresh are likely due
  3. The manager gets a weekly list of who to contact with a ready message
  4. More repeat orders without tracking clients in a notebook
Takes off: looks like the team keeps track of repeat needs by memory or spreadsheets

Where to start: One week, one category (for example laptops): you share 20–30 past requests with the quotes you sent, current price lists and your quote template. I set up AI to draft quotes for new requests and your manager compares them with their own.

What AI won't fix. AI won't negotiate a batch price with a vendor or promise a lead time a supplier hasn't confirmed, that stays with your people. If price lists are outdated, the drafts will be too, so they need regular updates.
